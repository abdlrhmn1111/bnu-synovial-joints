Definition

A synovial joint is a freely movable (diarthrotic) joint in which the articulating bone surfaces are separated by a fluid-filled synovial cavity and enclosed within a joint capsule.

  • Articular surfaces covered by hyaline cartilage
  • Synovial cavity containing synovial fluid
  • Fibrous joint capsule
  • Inner synovial membrane
  • Allows a wide range of movements

Examples: knee, hip, shoulder, and elbow joints.

Structure

Articular cartilage

  • Hyaline cartilage layer that is 2–4 mm thick
  • Poor vascularization and innervation; the tissue cannot repair itself effectively
  • Major macromolecules are type II collagen and aggrecan; abundant water contributes to resilience to pressure
  • Fed by diffusion from the synovial fluid in the joint cavity
  1. Superficial zoneresists shear
  2. Middle zone
  3. Deep zoneresists compression
  4. Calcified zonetied to the bone by the tidemark

Capsule and synovial fluid

  • The fibrous capsule is thick and strong
  • The inner membrane produces fluid that reduces friction, provides nutrition, and cushions the joints
  • The liquid fills the joint cavity and is viscous due to hyaluronic acid and lubricin proteins that reduce friction

Ligaments

  • Made of dense regular connective tissue, connects the bones to each other and prevents abnormal movement
  • Three types: capsular, extracapsular, and intracapsular (the ACL and PCL are examples of the latter)
  • They contain sensory nerves that respond to stretch and provide proprioception

Accessory structures

Include menisci and discs, labra, bursae, tendon sheaths, and fat pads that supplement the joints.

Stability

Imposes of the surfaces in the joint, ligaments, and muscle tone and tendons provide stability (the last of which is the most important for the shoulder and knee).

Classification

Synovial joints are freely movable joints characterized by a joint cavity filled with synovial fluid. They provide mobility, flexibility, and support for body movement.

Movements

Synovial joints are the most freely movable joints in the body. Their structure allows a wide variety of smooth movements.
